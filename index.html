How this works

You are moved passively through a field of drifting points. Nothing marks your position, and the points are short-lived so none can be used as a landmark. Your speed varies continuously, so counting seconds will not give you the distance — you have to integrate the motion you see.

Angle: reproduce the turn you were just given. Distance: reproduce the distance you were just moved. Homing: turn until you face where you started. Renav: face the start, then travel back and stop on it — direction and distance combined, so it is the hardest. Tap a type to include or exclude it; harder ones unlock as the level rises, but your choice always wins.

Controls. Turn by dragging the view, or with ←→ (↑↓ pitch in 3D, QE for the fourth axis in 4D). Move forward by holding Hold to move, Space or W, and back up with Back or S if you overshoot. Confirm with Enter. The line under the buttons always shows what is available right now.

Path. Passive carries you along a fixed route. Active lets you steer to a series of markers that vanish once reached, so you generate the path yourself — but the engine still owns your speed, so timing your own key presses never gives you the distance.

The staircase is a 2-down/1-up transformed rule (Levitt, 1971): two correct in a row makes it harder, one wrong makes it easier, which holds you at about 71% success. Step size halves at the 2nd, 4th and 6th reversal, and the threshold estimate is the mean of the last six reversals. It adapts legs per path, speed variability, turn spread, acceleration, flow density, dot lifetime and delay — legs keep growing, so there is no ceiling. Use − and + to move the level yourself and let it carry on from there, pin the world to 2D, 3D or 4D at any time, or switch to Manual to set every parameter by hand.

The fourth dimension. Where you sit along the fourth axis is shown the way 4D visualisation research does it — Aflalo and Graziano used a warm/cold colour cue for the extra axis, and early higher-dimensional rendering leaned on colour, texture, shadow and lighting for the same job. Dots ahead of you along w turn amber, dots behind turn blue, level ones stay pale, and 4D perspective shrinks whatever is far along w. The optional w-pulse adds a brightness wave whose phase follows w, so translating along that axis sweeps a ripple through the field; it is off by default, since a flickering display can be mistaken for motion and motion is exactly what this task asks you to read.

Your data. Everything is stored in this browser on this device. There are no network requests anywhere in this file. Reset session erases it.
